# State Capacity Lens

Use this framework instead of the crude "big government vs. small government" framing. The Indian state is simultaneously overreaching in some domains and absent in others — blanket statements about state size miss the actual problem.

## The Four Dimensions

### 1. Expenditure
Is the state spending too much or too little in this domain?
- Not all spending is equal: implementation leakage, administrative overhead, and distortionary effects mean the gross figure overstates welfare delivered.
- Questions: Is the budget allocation adequate? Is it reaching the intended beneficiaries? What is the leakage rate?

### 2. Size
Is the bureaucracy too large or too small?
- India's public administration is simultaneously overstaffed (frontline positions in outdated functions) and understaffed (regulatory agencies, technical specialists).
- Questions: Does the agency have enough people? Do those people have the right skills? Is the span of control manageable?

### 3. Capability
Does the state have the right skills and systems?
- Capability is not just headcount — it includes technical skills, IT systems, inter-agency coordination mechanisms, and institutional memory.
- Questions: Can the implementing agency actually do this? What training or system upgrades are required? Has this agency successfully delivered comparable tasks?

### 4. Ambition
Is the state trying to do too much, or has it abdicated core responsibilities?
- Overreach: the state tries to regulate or deliver in areas where markets or communities are more efficient.
- Abdication: the state withdraws from core public goods (health, education, infrastructure) where market provision fails.
- Questions: Should the state be doing this at all? Is there a market or community alternative? Or is this a genuine public good where only the state can act?

---

## How to Apply

At **Step 1** (define the problem): Use the four dimensions to diagnose the root cause.
> "This is not a spending problem — the budget allocation is adequate. This is a capability problem: the regulatory agency lacks the technical staff to enforce the rules it has already written."

At **Step 7** (decide): Check the capability dimension before recommending.
> "This recommendation requires the implementing agency to do X. Currently, they lack capability Y. The recommendation must include a capacity-building component, or it will fail at implementation."

---

## Federal Structure Check

Before recommending, ask:
- Is this a **Union List** subject (Parliament has exclusive jurisdiction)?
- Is this a **State List** subject (State Legislature has exclusive jurisdiction)?
- Is this a **Concurrent List** subject (both can legislate; Central law prevails in case of conflict)?

Many Indian policy failures result from recommending a Union-level solution to a State-list problem, or ignoring the fiscal relationship between Union transfers and State implementation.
